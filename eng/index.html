📐 Grammar Explained

Present Simple · Present Continuous · Adverbs of Frequency · Quantity Words

🟢 Present Simple

Use it for habits, routines, facts, and general truths.

✅ [Subject] + verb (base)  →  She works every day.
❌ [Subject] + don't/doesn't + verb  →  He doesn't smoke.
❓ Do/Does + [Subject] + verb?  →  Do you like coffee?
SubjectPositiveNegativeQuestion
I / You / We / Theyworkdon't workDo you work?
He / She / Itworksdoesn't workDoes she work?
⚠️ 3rd person singular (he/she/it) — spelling rules Most verbs → add -s: work → works, eat → eats
Verbs ending in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -o → add -es: go → goes, watch → watches, fix → fixes
Verbs ending in consonant + y → change y to -ies: study → studies, try → tries
Irregular: have → has, be → is
📌 When to use Present Simple • Habits & routines: I wake up at 7am. She drinks tea every morning.
• Permanent situations: He lives in London. I work at a hospital.
• General truths & facts: Water boils at 100°C. The sun rises in the east.
• Schedules & timetables: The train leaves at 8:15.
• With adverbs of frequency: She always brushes her teeth twice a day.

Examples:

✅ Positive❌ Negative❓ Question
I play tennis on Sundays.I don't play football.Do you play tennis?
She reads every night.She doesn't read magazines.Does she read books?
They study English.They don't study French.Do they study hard?
He goes to the gym.He doesn't go running.Does he go often?

🔵 Present Continuous

Use it for actions happening right now or temporary situations.

✅ [Subject] + am/is/are + verb-ing  →  She is working right now.
❌ [Subject] + am/is/are + not + verb-ing  →  I'm not sleeping.
❓ Am/Is/Are + [Subject] + verb-ing?  →  Are you listening?
SubjectPositiveNegativeQuestion
II'm workingI'm not workingAm I working?
He / She / ItShe's workingShe isn't workingIs she working?
You / We / TheyThey're workingThey aren't workingAre they working?
⚠️ -ing spelling rules Most verbs → add -ing: eat → eating, sleep → sleeping
Verbs ending in -e → drop e, add -ing: make → making, write → writing
Short verbs (CVC) → double final consonant + -ing: run → running, sit → sitting, swim → swimming
Verbs ending in -ie → change to -ying: lie → lying, die → dying
🚫 Stative verbs — NEVER use with Present Continuous These verbs describe states, not actions — they don't take -ing:
Feelings: love, hate, like, want, need, prefer
Thinking: know, believe, understand, think (= believe), remember, forget
Senses: see, hear, smell, taste, seem, appear
Possession: have (= own), own, belong, contain
❌ I am knowing the answer.  ✅ I know the answer.

🟢 Present Simple

  • I drink coffee every morning. (habit)
  • She works at a school. (permanent)
  • He always walks to work. (routine)
  • They don't eat meat. (general)

🔵 Present Continuous

  • I'm drinking coffee right now. (now)
  • She's working from home this week. (temporary)
  • He's walking to the station. (now)
  • They're trying a vegan diet. (temporary)
📌 Key time expressions Present Simple: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day/week, on Mondays, twice a day
Present Continuous: now, right now, at the moment, currently, today, this week, Look! Listen!

🎯 Adverbs of Frequency

They tell us how often something happens. Used mainly with Present Simple.

always
100%
usually
~80%
often
~65%
sometimes
~40%
rarely
~15%
never
0%
📌 Position Rules Rule 1: Before the main verb → I always eat breakfast. She never drinks coffee.
Rule 2: After "to be" → He is always late. They are usually on time.
Rule 3: Sometimes can also go at the start or end → Sometimes I walk to work. I go to the gym sometimes.
AdverbExample sentence
alwaysShe always brushes her teeth before bed.
usuallyI usually take the bus to work.
oftenWe often cook at home.
sometimesHe sometimes forgets his keys.
rarely / seldomThey rarely eat fast food.
hardly everI hardly ever watch TV.
neverShe never drinks alcohol.
⚠️ Never use "not" with "never", "rarely" or "hardly ever" ❌ I don't never eat meat.  ✅ I never eat meat.
❌ She doesn't rarely go out.  ✅ She rarely goes out.

⚖️ Quantity Words

These words tell us how much or how many. The choice depends on whether the noun is countable or uncountable.

WordCountable nounsUncountable nounsExample
many✅ Yes❌ NoThere are many chairs.
much❌ No✅ YesThere isn't much water.
a lot of / lots of✅ Yes✅ YesShe has a lot of friends / money.
some✅ (affirmative)✅ (affirmative)I need some eggs / milk.
any✅ (negative/question)✅ (negative/question)Is there any bread? / I don't have any eggs.
a few✅ Yes❌ NoI have a few questions.
a little❌ No✅ YesCan I have a little sugar?
enough✅ Yes✅ YesWe have enough time / cups.
too many✅ Yes❌ NoThere are too many people.
too much❌ No✅ YesI eat too much sugar.
no✅ Yes✅ YesThere is no time / There are no seats.
📌 some vs. any — the key rule Use some in: affirmative sentences + offers + requests
→ I have some milk. Would you like some tea? Can I have some water?
Use any in: negative sentences + questions
→ I don't have any milk. Do you have any questions?
📌 a few vs. a little — memory trick a few = a small number (countable) → a few books, a few minutes, a few friends
a little = a small amount (uncountable) → a little time, a little money, a little patience
Both mean "not a lot, but enough" — they are positive in tone.
few (without "a") = almost none → negative tone → Few people understand this.

📚 A Day in the Life

A connected story using all the grammar and vocabulary — read it to see everything in context

Chapter 1

Tom's Morning Routine

Tom is a plumber. He wakes up every morning at 6:30. He always gets up immediately — he never stays in bed. He's one of those people. First, he has a shower, then he brushes his teeth. He's very careful about his teeth. Then he gets dressed in his work clothes — usually blue trousers and a grey shirt.

✅ He wakes up at 6:30. (habit → Present Simple)
✅ He always gets up immediately. (adverb of frequency → before the verb)
✅ He never stays in bed. (never → before the verb, no "not" needed)

After getting dressed, Tom goes to the kitchen. He usually makes a strong coffee and eats some toast. Not much — just a little butter and some jam. He doesn't have much time in the morning, and there aren't many things he enjoys eating before 7am.

✅ a little butter (butter = uncountable → a little)
✅ not much time (time = uncountable → much)
✅ not many things (things = countable → many)
· · ·
Chapter 2

The Kitchen Problem

At 7:30, Tom leaves the house. But today is different. His phone is ringing. It's Mrs. Davies from number 42. "Tom! My kitchen sink is blocked and water is leaking everywhere! Can you come?"

✅ His phone is ringing. (happening RIGHT NOW → Present Continuous)
✅ Water is leaking. (happening right now → Present Continuous)
Note: "ring" is not a stative verb → we CAN use -ing form

Tom often gets calls like this. People always have problems with their pipes. He turns his van around and drives to Mrs. Davies' house. She is standing at the door, looking very worried. She looks nervous and stressed.

Tom walks in. He sees the problem immediately: the drain is blocked. There's too much grease in the pipe — someone has been pouring cooking oil down the sink. This always causes problems.

✅ She is standing at the door. (in progress, right now)
✅ too much grease (grease = uncountable → too much)
✅ This always causes problems. (general truth → Present Simple)
· · ·
Chapter 3

Tom Gets to Work

Tom opens his toolbox. He has a few different tools for this type of job: a drain snake, a plunger, and some special cleaning fluid. He rarely uses chemicals — he prefers mechanical solutions. But today the blockage is too difficult for simple tools.

✅ a few tools (tools = countable → a few)
✅ He rarely uses chemicals. (rarely → before the main verb)
❌ He doesn't rarely use chemicals. (WRONG — double negative!)

While Tom is working, Mrs. Davies is making tea. She usually offers tea to anyone who comes to her house. She is talking to her neighbour, Mr. Green, who is an electrician. Mr. Green sometimes helps Tom with jobs when a house needs both plumbing and electrical work.

✅ While Tom is working ... (both temporary actions happening simultaneously)
✅ She is making tea. (right now)
✅ She usually offers tea. (habit → Present Simple)
· · ·
Chapter 4

More Than Just Plumbing

Mrs. Davies has quite a big house. There's a living room, a large kitchen, three bedrooms, two bathrooms, and a small study. She also has a beautiful garden and a garage. The house has enough space for a family, but she lives alone.

While Tom is fixing the sink, he notices something: the tap in the bathroom is also leaky. The boiler makes a strange noise. The curtains in the living room are dirty. The house needs a lot of work.

✅ enough space (enough works with both countable and uncountable)
✅ a lot of work (work = uncountable → a lot of)
Vocabulary: tap, boiler, curtains, bathroom, living room ← house vocabulary!

"You don't need many tradespeople — you need the right ones." — Tom (probably)

Tom calls Mr. Green: "Are you free this afternoon? I'm finishing here around 2pm. The boiler is making a strange noise and I think there's an electrical issue with the switch in the kitchen."

Mr. Green says he's usually free on Thursday afternoons. He doesn't often refuse extra work. He's a hard-working, friendly man — a bit quiet but always polite.

· · ·
Chapter 5

The End of the Day

By 4pm, Tom is tired but happy. The sink is fixed, the tap is repaired, and the boiler is running smoothly again. Mrs. Davies looks relieved. She always pays on time — she's one of his best customers.

Tom drives home. He rarely cooks on weekdays — he's usually too tired. He sometimes orders pizza, but today he has some pasta and vegetables in the fridge. He makes a simple dinner. He doesn't have much energy left.

✅ He rarely cooks on weekdays. (habit + adverb of frequency)
✅ He sometimes orders pizza. (sometimes before the verb)
✅ He has some pasta. (affirmative → some)
✅ He doesn't have much energy. (negative + uncountable → much)

At 10pm, Tom goes to bed. He always reads a little before sleeping — usually a few pages of a thriller. He's never tired of reading. He falls asleep quickly. Tomorrow, he wakes up at 6:30 again.

✅ a little before sleeping (a little → small amount, uncountable idea)
✅ a few pages (pages = countable → a few)
✅ He's never tired of reading. (never after "to be")
